## ACT 1 — 1998, LLB SACLAY

KATY TWIN:
A student at IUT de Cachan, electrical engineering, industrial computing. An internship at the Laboratoire Léon Brillouin, CEA Saclay. The team: building control software for neutron spectrometers. Object-oriented C++. CORBA. A framework that would later look suspiciously like something called TANGO.

Her mentor: Alain Buteau. Brilliant engineer. Great teacher. And the author of one of the most ambitious acronyms in the history of scientific computing.

*(beat)*

He named his control program POOPS.

**[KATY REAL, deadpan, off to the side]**

KATY REAL:
It stood for something respectable, I promise.

**[TWIN]**

KATY TWIN:
*Programme Orienté Objet Pour le Pilotage de Spectromètre.* Object-oriented programme for spectrometer control. Logical. Descriptive. Also — and this part is true — a quiet tribute to his son's favorite stuffed toy.

Alain was proud of it. Very proud. Right up until an English colleague gently pointed out that presenting "POOPS" at an international conference in the United Kingdom might raise a few eyebrows.

*(beat)*

The programme was renamed before departure. Some acronyms are best left at home.

## ACT 2 — THE EXPIRED ID CARD

KATY TWIN:
Year 2000. NOBUGS 3 — New Opportunities for Better User Group Software — Daresbury Laboratory, UK. A paper on object-oriented spectrometer control, CORBA, C++. Looking back, remarkably close to what TANGO would become.

She was nineteen. First international conference. Technical English. In front of real scientists.

*(beat)*

Charles de Gaulle airport. Alain, a colleague named Nathalie, and her. At the gate, Alain reaches for his ID card.

Expired.

**[KATY REAL, cutting in, still deadpan]**

KATY REAL:
The UK isn't in the Schengen zone. No boarding for Alain.

**[TWIN]**

KATY TWIN:
So a nineteen-year-old with technical English still very much under construction, and a colleague with nothing but a driving licence and remarkable composure, got on that plane alone. Nathalie handled the logistics. Katy handled the poster session — and somehow explained object-oriented spectrometer control to a room of engineers who were, very politely, pretending to understand her.

She's never forgotten that conference. Neither has Alain.

*(beat)*

Lesson learned that trip, and it stuck for twenty-five years: the paper is the excuse. The real work happens in the corridors, over bad coffee. The community is the product.

## ACT 3 — THE CAREER, FAST

KATY TWIN:
Fast version, because we have places to be.

Two thousand one to two thousand four: software consultant, ALTEN. HMI development for digital decoders. First contact with Java.

Two thousand four to twenty seventeen: research engineer, Synchrotron SOLEIL. Thirteen years with TANGO Controls, across the accelerator and every beamline. HMI specialist. On-call support, twenty-four seven. Coordinator of the MAX-IV / SOLEIL collaboration.

Twenty seventeen onward: CEA Saclay, IRFU. EPICS expert. Product owner of MUSCADE — ninety servers, forty-plus projects, seven departments. Cited, eventually, by Bob Dalesio himself, at Oak Ridge.

**[KATY REAL]**

KATY REAL:
Twenty-five years. Two ecosystems. One translator in the middle.

## ACT 4 — WHY TRANSMISSION MATTERS

**[TWIN, tone shifts — sincere, no jokes here]**

KATY TWIN:
Here's the part that isn't funny.

The people who built EPICS and TANGO are approaching retirement. Bob Dalesio. Jens Meyer. Jeff Hill just stepped back. Marty Kraimer passed away in twenty twenty-two.

The decisions made in the late eighties and nineties — the architecture, the tradeoffs, the reasons behind the reasons — don't live in the documentation. They live in the people.

*(beat)*

That's what this podcast is actually for. Catching those stories before they're gone. Handing them to whoever's next.

**[KATY REAL, quiet]**

KATY REAL:
Alone, we go faster.

**[TWIN]**

KATY TWIN:
Together, we go further.

*(beat, small smile)*

Someone said that at a conference once. It got quoted back to her by the co-creator of EPICS. She didn't expect that to happen either.
